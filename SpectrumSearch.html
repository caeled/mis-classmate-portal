Spectrum Search

Search that learns how your mind takes in information, then changes how results are shown. Prototype v0.5.

After a few searches, the app will tell you, in plain language, what pattern it noticed, and ask before it adapts anything. You can also open “What has the app noticed?” at any time. Every setting can be changed by hand.

Here is what I noticed. May I adapt?

Nothing changes unless you say yes. Saying no costs nothing; the app keeps working the standard way.

How my results are shown

These are the presentation settings. The app can suggest them from your searching; you can always set them yourself.

Definitions and direct facts rank above commentary, roundups, and opinion.
Show the top 3 results. Everything else waits behind a “show more” button, so tangents do not compete for attention.
How much of each result you see. Level 1: headlines only. Level 2: one line each. Level 3: the full summary. Level 4: full summary plus context — what kind of source it is and where it comes from.
Results shown as an ordered sequence with a marked starting point, instead of an unordered pile.

Saved topics

A topic is a search thread you can return to. It remembers the questions you asked in it and how results were shown for it. Click a topic to pick the thread back up with its own settings. While a topic is active, your searches join its thread.

Privacy, plainly: your pattern and settings live only in this browser (localStorage). Reading time is measured only as the gap between your click on a result and your return to this page; nothing about the pages you visit is read. The only thing that leaves this machine is the search query itself, sent to DuckDuckGo, Wikipedia, and Google's suggestion service (and to Google Custom Search, if a search key is configured), the same as using them directly.
