  • Direct quote: add the page: (Laudon, 2024, p. 112). Quotes of 40+ words become a block quote: indented, no quotation marks, citation after the final period.
  • Paraphrase: page numbers are optional but welcome. Paraphrasing still requires a citation; changing a few words of someone else's sentence is not paraphrasing.
  • Secondary source: (Original, 1998, as cited in Summarizer, 2024). Cite the source you actually read in the reference list.

Reference list: the entries you will actually use

Journal article

Author, A. A., Author, B. B., & Author, C. C. (Year). Title of article: Subtitle in sentence case. Title of Journal in Title Case, Volume(Issue), pages. https://doi.org/xxxx

Example: Tarafdar, M., Tu, Q., & Ragu-Nathan, T. S. (2011). Impact of technostress on end-user satisfaction and performance. Journal of Management Information Systems, 27(3), 303–334. https://doi.org/10.2753/MIS0742-1222270311

Book

Author, A. A. (Year). Title of book: Subtitle in sentence case (Edition, if not the first). Publisher.

Chapter in an edited book

Chapter Author, A. A. (Year). Title of chapter. In E. E. Editor (Ed.), Title of book (pp. xx–xx). Publisher.

Webpage

Author, A. A. or Organization. (Year, Month Day). Title of page. Site Name. https://full-url (no period after the URL; add a retrieval date only if the content is designed to change, like a wiki).

Report or white paper

Organization Name. (Year). Title of report (Report No. xxx, if given). https://full-url

Video

Uploader or Author. (Year, Month Day). Title of video [Video]. YouTube. https://full-url

AI tool (per APA's guidance: cite it like software)

Company. (Year). Model name (Version, if known) [Large language model]. https://url-of-the-tool

Example: Anthropic. (2026). Claude [Large language model]. https://claude.ai

In text: (Anthropic, 2026). If you quote or paraphrase a model's output, cite it the way you would any source you used, and follow your instructor's rules on AI use and disclosure, which vary by course.

Paper mechanics (student papers)

  • Title page: title (bold, centered, upper half of the page), your name, university, course, instructor, due date. Student papers do not need a running head.
  • Body: double-spaced throughout, 1-inch margins, one approved font consistently (e.g., 12-pt Times New Roman, 11-pt Calibri or Arial).
  • Headings: Level 1 centered and bold; Level 2 flush left and bold; Level 3 flush left, bold, italic. Do not label headings "Introduction"; the first heading after the title is the paper's opening section under its own title.
  • Reference list: starts on a new page, headed "References" (bold, centered). Entries alphabetized by first author's surname, with a hanging indent of 0.5 inch.
  • Numbers: spell out zero through nine in prose; use numerals for 10 and above, and for any number in a table, statistic, or measurement.
  • Capitalization in references: article and book titles in sentence case; journal names and volume numbers in title case. This asymmetry is the single most-missed rule.

The five mistakes that cost the most points

  • A period after a DOI or URL. There is never one.
  • Reference entries with authors' first names spelled out. APA uses initials only.
  • "et al." in the reference list. The list spells out up to 20 authors in full.
  • Citing a source in text that never appears in the list, or listing sources never cited. The two must match exactly.
  • Copy-pasting a citation from a database or an AI tool without checking it. Generated citations can have real journals, wrong years, and invented DOIs. Verify in Crossref before you submit (the Research Workbench walks through this).
