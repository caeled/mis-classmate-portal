A repeatable literature review

  • Frame a question: population or setting, intervention or technology, comparison, and outcome. Example: how does source-grounded AI change policy-search accuracy for university staff?
  • Build synonym groups: ("information systems" OR MIS) AND (adoption OR acceptance) AND ("small business" OR SME). Adapt operators to each database; not all search engines parse them the same way.
  • Search at least one MIS-focused collection (AIS eLibrary), one broad scholarly index, and primary technical documentation where relevant. Record exact queries, filters, and dates.
  • Screen titles and abstracts against criteria chosen before reading results. Record exclusions and distinguish unavailable full text from irrelevant studies.
  • Read full papers and trace both references and citing works. Deduplicate by DOI and title in a citation manager.
  • Extract setting, sample, method, outcome, effect size if reported, limitations, and conflicts of interest. Separate preprints, peer-reviewed studies, and vendor reports.
  • Synthesize disagreements and evidence gaps. Cite the source you actually read and disclose AI assistance according to course rules.

From wiki browsing to a defensible claim

A wiki is a useful concept map, not the endpoint of research. Read its references, revision history, and discussion page. Open the source behind a claim; the source may be older, narrower, or less conclusive than the summary. Keep a stable revision URL when your argument concerns what the wiki said at a particular time.

Wikidata exercise: choose ten software products, inspect their developer and license statements, then record which statements have references. An absent statement is missing data, not proof that a property does not exist. Use the query service guide to learn how structured relationships differ from keyword search.

Evidence matrix and search log

Use these columns in your notes: claim ID; source title; author/year; DOI/URL; source type; page/section; supporting evidence; limitations; verification date; disposition. Keep the full citation separate from your interpretation.

Suggested inclusion rule for an MIS adoption review: empirical studies of organizational users, with a described method and an adoption outcome. Exclude opinion-only pieces from the evidence synthesis while retaining them as background. This is a teaching example, not a universal review protocol.

When an AI suggests a paper: resolve the title or DOI in Crossref, open the publication, and verify the claimed result. A real DOI attached to the wrong title is still an incorrect citation.
